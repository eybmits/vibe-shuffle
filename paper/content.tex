\section{Introduction}

People use music to maintain or change how they feel, and emotional fit affects
listening satisfaction~\cite{juslin2008}. Most streaming recommenders, however,
learn long-term taste rather than react to a listener's current state. Affective
computing offers facial and autonomic signals, but these measurements are noisy,
privacy-sensitive, and easy to overinterpret.

Vibe Shuffle is a browser-based research prototype for testing a narrow claim:
does a transparent state-adaptive song policy produce higher perceived mood fit
than Random selection from the same controlled pool? The prototype represents
both listener and song in Russell's Valence--Arousal space~\cite{russell1980,
posner2005}. It does not claim to recover objective or clinical emotion.

The contribution is an auditable end-to-end artifact: client-side signal
processing, a frozen 100-track catalog, an explicit nearest-track policy, a
participant-blinded crossover protocol, and a row-level CSV audit trail. Raw
camera frames and heart-rate notifications are not uploaded by the application;
derived measurements and pseudonymous study metadata are exported only through
a deliberate local download.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{System overview}

Vibe Shuffle is a React/Vite single-page application without an application
backend. A camera channel supplies horizontal Valence, an optional Bluetooth
Heart Rate Service channel supplies vertical Arousal, and a 60\,s listening
window is summarized into one listener coordinate. The next song is selected
from a fixed catalog and played with Spotify's Web Playback SDK. The participant
then supplies ratings before the protocol advances.

MediaPipe and Spotify remain external dependencies: model assets, authentication,
and playback require network traffic. Signal inference and CSV construction occur
in the browser.

\subsection{Facial display and Valence}

At a target interval of 120\,ms, MediaPipe Face Landmarker~\cite{mediapipe2019}
returns 52 blendshape activations. The application reduces smile, cheek-squint,
frown, brow, eye, jaw, and mouth cues to custom \emph{Happy}, \emph{Sad}, and
\emph{Tense} display scores. MediaPipe does not output these emotion labels; the
mapping is a hand-specified, FACS-inspired heuristic in the application source.

A 14\,s calibration captures the participant's current facial reference. A slow
running baseline and exponential smoothing reduce resting-morphology and
single-frame effects. If $\bar H$, $\bar S$, and $\bar T$ are the mean
baseline-relative rule scores for a listening window, Valence is

\begin{equation}
V=\operatorname{clip}_{[0.05,0.95]}
\left[0.5+1.05\left(\bar H-0.55\bar S-0.45\bar T\right)\right].
\end{equation}

No detected face sets $V=0.5$. Whole-frame movement and rhythmic head motion
currently add positive evidence to the facial channel. This encodes an
exploratory engagement assumption, not an established affect measurement, and
is treated as a limitation.

\subsection{Cardiac signal and Arousal}

The optional sensor exposes BPM and, when available, RR intervals: elapsed time
between successive cardiac beats. The application rejects RR values outside
300--2000\,ms and values that differ by more than 30\% from the previous accepted
interval. RMSSD measures short-term variability,

\begin{equation}
\operatorname{RMSSD}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{n-1}
\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(RR_{i+1}-RR_i)^2}.
\end{equation}

At least 20 accepted RR intervals are required for the full HR/RMSSD estimate.
A 120\,s personal baseline stores median HR and median chunk-level RMSSD. Robust
spread is $\max(1.4826\operatorname{MAD},4\,\mathrm{BPM})$ for HR and
$\max(1.4826\operatorname{MAD},6\,\mathrm{ms})$ for RMSSD. The baseline-relative
scores are

\begin{align}
z_{HR} &= \frac{\operatorname{median}(HR_w)-\operatorname{median}(HR_b)}{s_{HR}},\\
z_{RMSSD} &= \frac{\operatorname{median}(RMSSD_b)-RMSSD_w}{s_{RMSSD}}.
\end{align}

Positive $z_{HR}$ and $z_{RMSSD}$ therefore both represent higher-Arousal
evidence. A deadband discards $|z|\leq0.25$ and caps larger magnitudes at three
spread units. With this transform $D$, the implemented physiological coordinate
is

\begin{equation}
A_{phys}=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}
\left[0.5+0.18\left(0.75D(z_{HR})+0.35D(z_{RMSSD})\right)\right].
\end{equation}

When RMSSD suggests higher Arousal but HR is at or below baseline, the RMSSD
weight is multiplied by 0.2. This conservative conflict rule limits unstable
short-window HRV. SDNN and a rhythm-coherence value are exported as diagnostics
but do not drive Arousal. The application receives processed RR data, not a raw
ECG waveform, and cannot identify every motion artifact.

\subsection{Fusion and windowing}

Facial display supplies $V$. Usable physiology supplies the Arousal base;
positive camera movement can add upward Arousal. Without usable physiology,
camera motion supplies upward-only Arousal. Without a face, $V=0.5$; without
either usable signal, the coordinate is $(0.5,0.5)$.

During confirmed playback, every change to the live fused state appends a point
to the trial buffer. At rating time, the application takes the arithmetic mean
of all valid buffered positions. The sampling is event-driven rather than fixed-
rate and the mean is not time-weighted. The export therefore includes the sample
count and actual listening duration so this approximation can be audited.

Splitting both axes at 0.5 yields four recommendation quadrants: Energetic
(high $V$, high $A$), Calm (high $V$, low $A$), Tense (low $V$, high $A$), and
Melancholic (low $V$, low $A$).

\subsection{Study catalog}

The frozen catalog contains 100 real Spotify track IDs and historical Valence,
Energy, Instrumentalness, and popularity values from the public Kaggle Spotify
Tracks Dataset~\cite{spotifydataset}. High-popularity entries were de-duplicated,
manually curated, and frozen at 25 tracks per quadrant. The final artifact is
enforced by a catalog integrity test; the repository does not claim a fully
automatic generator for the curatorial choices.

Song Valence supplies the horizontal coordinate and song Energy is treated as
the catalog-side counterpart of listener Arousal. This is a modeling assumption:
musical Energy and physiological Arousal are related constructs, not identical
measurements. The current catalog is mainstream rather than instrumental-only.

\subsection{Track selection}

Both conditions hard-exclude the current and previously played tracks. Random
ranks every remaining track with a deterministic pseudorandom score from track
ID and session seed; measured signals are logged but ignored.

Vibe maps the listener target $(V,A)$ to its quadrant, keeps unplayed tracks in
that quadrant, and minimizes Euclidean distance,

\begin{equation}
s^*=\arg\min_{s\in Q(V,A)}
\sqrt{(V_s-V)^2+(E_s-A)^2}.
\end{equation}

The seed resolves only an exact distance tie. If the quadrant is exhausted, the
method falls back to all remaining tracks. The first track uses the setup-time
coordinate; subsequent Vibe selections use the mean coordinate from the
preceding trial. The CSV records target, source, distance, and quadrant match.

\subsection{Validation protocol}

Every participant completes both conditions in a participant-blinded crossover:
one five-track Random block and one five-track Vibe block. Protocol 1 uses
Random--Vibe; Protocol 2 uses Vibe--Random. Odd/even participant numbers suggest
alternating orders, which the experimenter can override before start. This is
within-participant comparison with order counterbalanced across participants,
not double blinding.

Each track plays for up to 60\,s. An early \emph{Rate now} action is preserved in
the export. The mandatory prompt collects 7-point liking, 7-point mood fit, and
a categorical current-mood report. Mood fit is the primary outcome; liking is a
control for general song preference.

The proposed participant-level analysis averages the five mood-fit ratings per
condition and evaluates the paired Vibe-minus-Random differences. With a small
sample and ordinal responses, an exact paired Wilcoxon or paired permutation
test is the robust primary option; a paired $t$-test is a sensitivity analysis
when the difference distribution is sufficiently regular. Early and low-quality
trials should be handled by a rule fixed before inspecting the condition result.

\section{Artifact Output}

One local CSV row is created per rated track. It contains protocol and song
metadata, both ratings, categorical self-report, listening duration, fused state,
expression summaries, HR/RR/HRV summaries, selection audit fields, and quality
flags. Non-finite numeric values become blank cells rather than textual
\texttt{NaN} values.

The repository intentionally includes no participant dataset. This paper
documents the implemented system and analysis plan; it does not report an
inferential participant result.

\section{Discussion}

The artifact isolates the policy comparison by holding the song pool, playback
path, rating questions, and exposure count constant. Its transparent rules make
failures auditable and provide a concrete basis for an HCI study of implicit
adaptation.

The limits are substantial. Facial movement is not a direct readout of felt
emotion~\cite{barrett2019}; the custom weights were not learned from labeled
affect; HR/HRV reflects many non-emotional influences; and the personal baseline
captures relative change rather than absolute initial mood. Motion contributes
to both Valence and Arousal and can confound the two channels. The event-driven
window mean is not time-weighted. The fixed balanced catalog aids control but
limits ecological and catalog-scale generalization. Spotify Premium,
Development-mode allowlisting, browser permissions, and BLE support constrain
deployment.

The privacy claim is also bounded. The application has no study-data upload
endpoint and does not export raw frames or packets, but GitHub, MediaPipe, and
Spotify still receive ordinary service traffic. Exported participant numbers and
timestamps are pseudonymous, not anonymous, and require appropriate research-
data handling.

Future work should validate the mapping against a separate labeled dataset,
compare motion ablations, use a time-weighted fusion window, prespecify artifact
handling, and evaluate the recommendation effect with a larger sample and a
catalog not manually balanced by quadrant.

\section{Conclusion}

Vibe Shuffle is an open, browser-based testbed for comparing a transparent
state-adaptive music policy with Random selection under controlled stimuli. It
does not solve emotion recognition; it makes one rule-based recommendation
hypothesis explicit, measurable, and falsifiable through participant-rated mood
fit.
