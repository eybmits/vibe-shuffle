%%
%% The abstract is a short summary of the work to be presented in the

\begin{abstract}
Music is widely used to regulate emotion, yet mainstream recommenders optimise
long-run engagement rather than a listener's momentary state. We present
\emph{Vibe Shuffle}, a browser-based research application that estimates a
baseline-relative valence--arousal coordinate: facial blendshapes and custom
rules supply Valence, while optional heart rate and short-term heart-rate
variability supply Arousal. The adaptive policy selects the nearest unplayed
track in the matching quadrant of a fixed 100-track catalog. A participant-
blinded, counterbalanced crossover compares this policy with seeded Random
selection from the same pool. Each track yields 7-point mood-fit and liking
ratings plus a categorical mood report. Raw camera frames and heart-rate packets
remain in browser memory; the participant-triggered CSV contains derived signals,
ratings, pseudonymous identifiers, and timestamps. The open artifact makes the
heuristics, quality rules, selection mechanism, and limitations auditable.
\end{abstract}
