\begin{abstract}
Music recommendations usually reflect long-term taste, although listeners' needs can change from moment to moment. We built Vibe Shuffle, a browser prototype that maps facial cues and optional heart-rate information to a simple valence--arousal position and uses that position to select the next song. The rules are intentionally transparent and are not presented as an objective emotion detector. In a within-participant crossover study, 15 participants rated five Vibe-selected and five randomly selected tracks. Mean mood fit was 4.71 for Vibe and 4.15 for Random, a paired difference of 0.56 points, but the uncertainty interval included zero and the directional test was not significant. Liking was almost unchanged. The result is promising but inconclusive. We describe the complete signal and adaptation pipeline, identify reporting and implementation gaps, and outline the validation needed before real-world deployment.
\end{abstract}
