\begin{abstract}
Music recommenders model durable preferences, but track fit also depends on
current context. We study a baseline-relative next-song policy in
\emph{Vibe Shuffle}. Fixed heuristics express available facial cues relative
to a personal reference on the Valence axis and available heart rate and
beat-to-beat variability relative to a cardiac reference on the Arousal axis.
Measurements collected during a track form a trajectory whose mean defines
one of four recommendation regions. The policy selects the nearest unplayed
song in the corresponding Valence--Energy region of a fixed catalog. The
current export schema records the target, signal source, region, distance,
quality flags, and ratings while excluding raw sensor data. An exploratory
crossover left the effects on mood fit and liking unresolved. Evaluation is
limited to the fixed catalog; sensor validation, complete trial records, and a
larger preregistered study are needed.
\end{abstract}
