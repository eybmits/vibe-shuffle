\begin{abstract}
Music recommenders learn what listeners like in general, but the song that fits
depends on the moment. Vibe Shuffle asks whether within-session camera and
cardiac observations can make next-song selection more context-sensitive. At
the start of a session, the system initializes personal references for facial
and cardiac signals. Later facial and cardiac observations are expressed as
changes from those references and combined with movement and nodding summarized
over a recent camera window. Together, these observations form a short
trajectory in a valence--arousal plane without assigning the listener an emotion
label. The trajectory mean identifies a catalog region, and the nearest unplayed
song in that region is selected. An exploratory within-session crossover
compared Vibe Shuffle with Random selection from the same catalog. Mood-fit
ratings were higher on average under Vibe Shuffle while general liking differed
little on average, but both confidence intervals included zero, so no
recommendation benefit is established. The contribution is an inspectable
method that turns within-session multimodal observations into a single
song-selection decision, together with a blueprint for the study needed to test
it.
\end{abstract}
