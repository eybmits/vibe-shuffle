\begin{abstract}
Music recommenders model durable preferences, but track fit also depends on
current context. \emph{Vibe Shuffle} is a research project that studies a
baseline-relative approach to next-song selection using camera and cardiac
observations. Short personal reference periods transform facial cues into
relative Valence evidence and heart rate and beat-to-beat variability into
relative Arousal evidence. Measurements collected during a track form a
trajectory whose mean defines one of four recommendation regions. The policy
selects the nearest unplayed song in the corresponding Valence--Energy region
of a fixed catalog. The trial record contains the target, signal source, region,
distance, quality flags, and ratings while excluding raw sensor data. An
exploratory crossover left the effects on mood fit and liking unresolved.
Evaluation is limited to the fixed 100-track catalog; sensor validation and a
larger preregistered study are needed.
\end{abstract}
