\begin{abstract}
Music recommendation usually models durable preferences, although the track
that suits a listener may vary with the immediate context. \emph{Vibe Shuffle}
is a browser application that adapts the next-song choice from camera and
cardiac signals. Its output is a recommendation coordinate derived from changes
against short personal reference periods. Facial cues supply relative Valence
evidence; heart rate and beat-to-beat variability supply relative Arousal
evidence. During playback, these estimates form a trajectory in
Valence--Arousal space. The trajectory mean identifies one of four regions, and
the policy selects the nearest unplayed song in the corresponding
Valence--Energy region of a fixed 100-track catalog. A local export records the
target, region match, song distance, derived signals, and ratings. Raw camera
frames, Bluetooth packets, facial-reference values, and point-level
trajectories remain unexported. In an exploratory 15-participant crossover, the
Vibe--Random mood-fit difference was $+0.56$ on a seven-point scale (95\% CI
$[-0.21,1.33]$); the liking difference was $+0.09$ (95\% CI
$[-0.52,0.71]$). Both confidence intervals included zero. A larger study with
complete trial-level records is needed to estimate the policy's effect on mood
fit and liking.
\end{abstract}
