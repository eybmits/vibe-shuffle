\section{Conclusion}

In the 15-participant crossover, the estimated Vibe--Random difference was
$+0.56$ rating points for mood fit (95\% CI $[-0.21,1.33]$) and $+0.09$ points
for liking (95\% CI $[-0.52,0.71]$). Both intervals include zero. Within the
fixed catalog, the pilot leaves both effects unresolved and does not establish
that baseline-relative selection improves what listeners hear next.

Vibe Shuffle supplies a specified policy that can be tested and audited.
Personal references express camera and cardiac observations as relative
Valence and Arousal evidence without assigning emotion labels. The
trajectory-to-region-to-nearest-song rule turns those observations into a
reviewable selection, and the paired means quantify direction and uncertainty
against Random. The sensor mappings lack independent validation, while the
historical archive lacks trial rows, signal-path frequencies, and a code
revision tied to collection. Current tests cover the present source tree, not
the historical sessions.

A confirmatory study should validate both mappings, resolve the no-signal
boundary, compare camera-only, cardiac-only, fused, and Random conditions,
evaluate time-weighted trajectory aggregation, preserve complete records, and
preregister the target effect and analysis. A changing-catalog platform study
can then test generalization. Vibe Shuffle makes the hypothesis precise and
auditable; evidence that baseline-relative sensing improves what plays next
remains to be produced.
