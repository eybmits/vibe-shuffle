\section{Conclusion}

Vibe Shuffle uses baseline-relative camera and cardiac observations to select
the next song. It tracks recent movement in Valence--Arousal space, averages the
trajectory, maps that point to one of four regions, and chooses the nearest
eligible song. The exported target, region, and distance identify the rule used
for each recommendation.

In the 15-participant crossover, average mood fit was $0.56$ points higher under
Vibe (95\% CI $[-0.21,1.33]$), and liking was $0.09$ points higher (95\% CI
$[-0.52,0.71]$). The current-mood reports were subjective ratings, and the
software produced no objective emotion label. The stored target, signal-source
summary, region, and song distance document the final decision; the export
omits point-level trajectory samples.

A larger preregistered study should validate the sensor mappings, use
time-weighted fusion, retain complete trial records, and include signal
ablations. The relevant outcome is whether a personal, inspectable adjustment
improves the perceived fit of what plays next.
