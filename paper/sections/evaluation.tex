\section{Results and Limitations}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\subsection{Mood-fit estimate and uncertainty}

Mood fit averaged 4.71 under Vibe and 4.15 under Random
(Table~\ref{tab:pilot}). The paired mean difference was $+0.56$ rating points
with a 95\% confidence interval from $-0.21$ to $1.33$. The supplied
directional paired test yielded $t(14)=1.56$, $p=.070$; the signed-rank
sensitivity check yielded $W^+=74.0$, $p=.088$. The standardized paired effect
was $d_z=0.40$. Eight participant/session means were higher under Vibe, one was
tied, and six were lower. The 95\% confidence interval includes effects on
either side of zero. Figure~\ref{fig:pilot} plots the paired participant means
and Vibe--Random differences.

Liking averaged 4.56 under Vibe and 4.47 under Random. The paired difference
was $+0.09$ with a 95\% confidence interval from $-0.52$ to $0.71$,
$t(14)=0.32$, $p=.375$, and $W^+=58.5$, $p=.353$. The liking confidence
interval includes zero; the study did not specify an equivalence margin.

\subsection{Computational verification}

The selection and signal-processing rules are pure JavaScript modules, allowing
deterministic verification without camera, wearable, or Spotify access. The 63
tests cover catalog size and region balance, CSV finiteness and quality flags,
facial-rule behavior, RR filtering, baseline-relative cardiac behavior, and
Random/Vibe selection. All tests pass for the current reported source tree.
They verify module-level computational behavior and contain no labeled affect
data, independent sensor ground truth, or end-to-end browser, MediaPipe,
Bluetooth, Spotify, and hardware checks. The archive does not identify the code
revision used during the pilot sessions.

Both policies used the same playback path, rating questions, exposure count,
and catalog. Their selected tracks could differ in artist, genre, language,
familiarity, and popularity.

\subsection{Measurement and study limits}

The facial and cardiac mappings have not been validated against ground truth.
Facial behavior cannot be reduced reliably to a universal readout of felt emotion
~\cite{barrett2019}. Vibe's rule weights were not learned or validated on a
labeled affect dataset. Lighting, pose, occlusion, morphology, culture, and
deliberate display can all change the output.

Heart rate and RMSSD also vary with respiration, posture, speaking, movement,
fitness, medication, contact quality, and vendor
processing~\cite{shaffer2017,quigley2024}. The listening window is short for
HRV, respiration is not measured, and the research system receives processed RR
intervals without access to raw peaks. Personal baselining adjusts
between-person levels but leaves within-session physiological changes and sensor
artifacts in the estimate.

Movement can affect the camera channel, raise heart rate, and introduce RR
artifacts at the same time. One behavior can be counted by both channels. The
trajectory mean weights coordinate-change events instead of elapsed time, and
the first selection may occur before both references are fully established.
Because sensing is optional, a Vibe selection can follow a camera-only,
cardiac-only, fused, or no-signal path. The archived participant means do not
report how often each path occurred.

At the exact no-signal center, current code paths disagree on which region
owns the boundary: the fallback label is Calm, whereas the numerical
greater-than-or-equal rule assigns $(0.5,0.5)$ to Energetic. This edge case
should be unified before a confirmatory study.

The study archive lacks raw trial rows, participant characteristics, recruitment
and compensation details, the exact sensor model, room conditions, consent
documentation, and the ethics procedure. The available archive therefore
cannot establish recruitment, ethics, sensor setup, or trial-level data
processing.

\subsection{Materials and evidence availability}

Source code, the frozen catalog, deterministic tests, figure scripts, and the
15 participant/session means are available at
\url{https://github.com/eybmits/vibe-shuffle}. The JavaScript dependency
versions are fixed in \texttt{package-lock.json}; the computational check is
run with Node.js 20 using \texttt{npm ci} followed by \texttt{npm test}. A final
submission should cite an immutable release of this archive.

The participant means and inferential summary were transcribed from the
supplied reports; their source-file hashes are documented in
\texttt{paper/RESULTS\_PROVENANCE.md}. Trial-level ratings, sensor streams,
administration records, and a code revision tied to pilot collection are not
present. The current export schema and tests therefore describe the current
implementation, not properties independently verified in the historical pilot
sessions.

\subsection{Next validation steps}

Validation should begin with controlled changes in expression, lighting,
posture, movement, and respiration applied to the frozen facial and cardiac
mappings. A confirmatory study should then require successful calibration,
timing, missing-data flags, and a complete ten-trial record. Before collecting
a larger crossover sample, preregister the meaningful mood-fit effect, sample
size, exclusions, primary paired analysis, order analysis, and
sensor-quality checks.
Base sample size on interval precision or a prespecified smallest relevant
effect~\cite{lakens2022}; the pilot estimate is too uncertain to serve as the
target.

Future protocols should specify whether the policy is intended to maintain,
intensify, or shift the recent region. The outcome measure should match that
stated goal.

A later study could evaluate the same policy on a larger, changing catalog. Its
protocol should record playback failures, unavailable features, catalog
changes, and recommendation coverage.
