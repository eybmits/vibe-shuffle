\section{Exploratory Crossover Study}
\label{sec:study}

\subsection{Protocol}

The study compares the perceived appropriateness of the relative adaptive
policy with Random selection from the same catalog. The protocol schedules
five tracks in each condition. Two masked protocols reverse block order, and
the study interface uses odd/even participant numbers to suggest an
assignment. In
participant-facing mode, the condition name is replaced by a protocol
identifier. The experimenter can access the view toggle, protocol controls, and
condition order in exported data. Masking depended on study administration and
was not enforced by the study interface. The study was not double blind.
Practice and carryover remain possible in the paired design; reversing block
order helps control those effects~\cite{greenwald1976}.

Each track plays for up to 60 seconds. Participants may select \emph{Rate now};
the actual duration and an early-rating flag are preserved. After each track,
the study interface collects a seven-point liking rating, a seven-point mood-fit
rating, and a categorical current-mood report. For this exploratory analysis,
mood fit is treated as the primary outcome because it measures the proposed
match directly. Liking is secondary and separates contextual fit from general
song preference. The prompts were \emph{How much do you like this song?}
(1, \emph{Don't like it}; 7, \emph{Love it}) and \emph{How well did it fit your
current mood?} (1, \emph{Not at all}; 7, \emph{Perfect fit}). The current-mood
prompt offered Energetic, Calm, Tense, Melancholic, and Neutral in randomized
order.

\subsection{Available evidence}

The supplied analysis report contains one five-trial condition mean per
participant/session for 15 participants, together with paired tests and
condition summaries. The archived analysis dataset contains 15
participant/session rows under generic analysis IDs. The protocol implies 75
planned ratings for each condition and outcome. Without the trial rows,
completeness, exclusions, and within-session
variation cannot be checked. Recruitment records, demographics, and the
complete study-administration file are also unavailable. The available files
limit the analysis to the 15 participant/session means. The human-study archive
remains incomplete. In particular, the archive does not show how often a Vibe
selection followed a camera-only, cardiac-only, fused, or no-signal path.

Paired differences are defined as Vibe minus Random. The analysis includes the
supplied participant/session means, condition means and standard deviations,
paired mean differences with two-sided 95\% confidence intervals, and the
originally supplied paired tests. No dated preregistration was available. We
report the paired estimates, 95\% confidence intervals, and supplied $p$-values
as exploratory results~\cite{nosek2018,cumming2014}; no confirmatory
significance threshold was specified. The participant/session five-trial mean
is the unit of analysis. Confidence intervals use the paired $t$ distribution
with 14 degrees of freedom. Figure~\ref{fig:pilot} adds 20,000 paired bootstrap
resamples with fixed seeds to show the distribution of the mean; its interval
bars remain the $t$ intervals, not bootstrap intervals.

The archived means are rounded to one decimal place. They reproduce the
condition means, paired means, $t$ statistics, and $t$ intervals to rounding.
Exact signed-rank results depend on the unavailable precision and tie
convention, so the paper retains the supplied signed-rank values.
